What is Tarati?

Tarati is a two-player abstract strategy game designed by mathematician George Spencer-Brown as a practical application of the calculus of distinctions from his book Laws of Form. Its structure is minimalist, its consequences deep: you move pieces across a board of 23 vertices arranged in concentric zones, flipping enemy pieces to your own colour and promoting your own toward victory.

There is also Tarati Six, a separate multiplayer variant for 2 to 6 players on an expanded board with cubic pieces.

How you win

A piece moves to an adjacent free vertex and flips every eligible enemy piece it approaches from outside (the pre-adjacency rule — the game's key tactic). Reach the opponent's base and your Cob is promoted to a Rok, which moves freely in any direction. You win by capturing the last enemy piece in a single move (Mit) or leaving the opponent with no legal move (Stalemit).

Laws of Form

Tarati is grounded in George Spencer-Brown's Laws of Form, a mathematically complete calculus built entirely on the concept of distinction. Tarati is the first serious digital game based on his work — a playable entry point into these ideas.
